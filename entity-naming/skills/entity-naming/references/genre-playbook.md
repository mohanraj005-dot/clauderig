# Genre playbook

Generating forty names inside one genre produces forty variations of the same
idea, and a founder who rejects the genre rejects all forty at once. Generating
across genres costs the same and gives them something to actually choose between.
Spread every wave over at least four of the genres below.

## The genres

| Genre | Shape | Examples from past rounds | Domain reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sanskrit-adjacent coined** | Sanskrit root + invented suffix, usually ending in -a | Trisendra, Prascenda, Tejasenda, Ojasendra | Clears well at 8-10 chars |
| **Coined abstract** | No etymology, built for sound alone | Ozarva, Kynexta, Vynosta, Vexosta | Best .com hit rate |
| **Anglo estate** | English landscape noun + "hold"/"crest"/"haven" | Lindenhold, Slatehold, Marlhaven, Rowanhold | Clears well, reads premium |
| **Sanskrit trio** | Encodes a number or a partnership | Triunnati, Trisangam, Trisrota, Tungava | Higher register-collision risk |
| **Blended founder** | Fragments of founder names fused | Damodeep (Danish + Deepak) | Very high .com hit rate, low distinctiveness |
| **Place / geo** | Locality or landmark reference | (dropped every round) | Almost always crowded on the register |

## What the domain market actually allows

This is the constraint that decides most rounds, so design generation around it
rather than discovering it forty candidates in:

- **4-5 characters: do not bother.** Every short .com is registered. Korva, Zento,
  Trevo, Verve, Zync — all failed. Generating short names wastes the round.
- **7-8 characters is the sweet spot.** Long enough to clear .com, short enough to
  read as one word on signage and in a handle.
- **10+ characters** clears easily but starts losing to abbreviation in daily use.

## Genre-specific cautions

**Place and geo names** were dropped in every round for the same reason: locality
names collide on the company register constantly, because every other business in
that locality had the same idea. Skip unless the founder specifically wants it.

**Blended founder names** clear domains easily precisely because nobody else would
coin them — but that same property makes them meaningless to customers, and they
age badly if a partner exits. Offer them, flag the exit risk.

**Sanskrit trio names** encoding "three partners" are charming and specific, and
they are the most likely to hit an existing register entry, because the same
device occurs to a lot of three-partner firms.

## Volume

Generate **10-12 per genre, 40-50 per wave**. Clearance is cheap and parallel;
generation is the cheap part too. What is expensive is the founder's attention,
so clear first and only show them what survived — a board of twenty cleared names
is useful, a list of fifty unchecked ones is noise.

## Always include a rescue lane

When a founder names something they love that is unavailable, generate 3-5
candidates that deliberately sit close to it — same prefix, same syllable count,
same rhythm. Trivista was gone; Trisendra was built to sit next to it. That lane
consistently produces the names that survive to the final round, because it is
the only lane anchored to demonstrated taste rather than inference.
